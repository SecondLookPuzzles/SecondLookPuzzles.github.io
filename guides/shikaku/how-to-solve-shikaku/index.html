How to Solve Shikaku Puzzles: Rules and Tips for Beginners

Updated 1 November 2026

Shikaku, also called Divide by Box, is a logic puzzle played on a grid with numbers scattered across it. Your goal is to divide the whole grid into rectangles, so that each rectangle contains exactly one number, and that number tells you how many squares the rectangle covers. You never need to guess: every rectangle can be worked out from the numbers.

In short. Start with the numbers that have the fewest possible shapes: big numbers, numbers near edges and corners, and numbers boxed in by their neighbours. Each rectangle you draw takes options away from the others.

What is Shikaku?

Shikaku comes from Japan, and its name means "square" or "box". You draw rectangles (squares count too) along the grid lines, until every square of the grid belongs to exactly one of them.

The only thing you need is to know which rectangles have a given size: a 6 can be 1×6, 2×3, 3×2 or 6×1. That is as close to maths as it gets.

Shikaku rules

  1. Divide the grid into rectangles along the grid lines.
  2. Each rectangle contains exactly one number.
  3. The number equals the number of squares in its rectangle.
  4. Every square of the grid belongs to exactly one rectangle.

Three Shikaku solving techniques

1. List the possible shapes

For each number, think of the rectangles with that many squares: a 4 can be 1×4, 2×2 or 4×1. Then check which of them fit around the number without leaving the grid.

2. Rule out shapes that cover another number

A rectangle can only hold one number. Any shape that would swallow a second number is impossible, and often just one shape is left.

3. Start with big numbers and tight spots

Large numbers have few places to fit, and numbers squeezed between the edge and other numbers have even fewer. Place those first, and the small numbers usually fall into place at the end.

Worked example: solving a 5×5 Shikaku step by step

Follow along with a pencil. The highlighted squares are the rectangle we have just drawn. Rows are numbered 1 to 5 from the top, and columns 1 to 5 from the left.

  1. Here is the puzzle. The numbers add up to 25, exactly the number of squares in the grid.

  2. The easiest one. A 1 is a rectangle on its own: just its square.

  3. The big number. In a 5×5 grid, the 9 has to be a 3×3 square. Of all the 3×3 squares containing it, only one avoids every other number: rows 1 to 3, columns 2 to 4.

  4. Squeezed against the edge. The 3 on the left can't go sideways (the 9 is there), and it can't reach down as far as the 2. So it covers rows 1 to 3 of column 1.

  5. The same on the right. The 3 on the right can't go up (the 1 is in the way) or sideways, so it runs down column 5, from row 2 to row 4.

  6. Rule out shapes. The 3 in row 4 can't go up into the 9, can't reach column 1 without swallowing the 2, and can't reach column 5. So it covers columns 2 to 4 of row 4.

  7. Finish. The 2 can now only go down, covering rows 4 and 5 of column 1. The 4 takes the rest of row 5. Every square is covered, and every rectangle holds one number.

Common Shikaku mistakes

  • Forgetting long, thin shapes. A 4 can be a 2×2 square, but also a straight line of four. Check both.
  • Drawing too early. If a number still has two possible shapes, leave it and come back later.
  • Leaving stray squares. Every square must end up inside a rectangle. A square that no number can reach means an earlier rectangle is wrong.
  • Guessing. If you're stuck, look for a square that only one number can reach. That number must cover it.

Shikaku questions and answers

Is Shikaku the same as Divide by Box?

Yes. Divide by Box is a common English name for the same puzzle, and the rules are identical.

Can a rectangle be a square?

Yes. A square is a rectangle, so a 4 can be a 2×2 block and a 9 can be a 3×3 block.

What size should a beginner start with?

A 5×5 or 6×6 grid is ideal for learning. Larger grids have bigger numbers and more choices, but the same techniques.

Does every Shikaku have only one solution?

A well-made puzzle does. The puzzles in this guide were checked by computer to confirm there is exactly one solution.
