How to Solve Queens Puzzles: Rules and Tips for Beginners

Updated 1 November 2026

Queens is a logic puzzle played on a square grid divided into coloured regions. Your goal is to place one queen in every row, every column and every colour region, so that no two queens touch, not even diagonally. You never need to guess: each queen can be worked out from the rules.

In short. Every queen you place rules out a whole row, a whole column, a whole region and the eight squares around it. Mark those squares with a small cross, and the remaining spaces soon show you where the next queen must go.

What is Queens?

Queens is a close cousin of the classic Star Battle puzzle and was made popular as a daily puzzle on LinkedIn. The name comes from chess, but you don't need to know chess to play: the queens never move, and they only need to stay out of each other's way.

A puzzle shows a grid, usually from 5×5 up to about 12×12, split into as many colour regions as there are rows. That's all you get. No numbers, no maths, just regions and a few simple rules.

Queens rules

  1. Place exactly one queen in every row.
  2. Place exactly one queen in every column.
  3. Place exactly one queen in every colour region.
  4. No two queens may touch, not even at a corner.

Three Queens solving techniques

1. Cross out everything a queen rules out

When you place a queen, put a small cross in every square of its row, its column and its region, and in the eight squares around it. None of those squares can hold a queen any more.

2. Look for regions squeezed into one row or column

If all of a region's open squares lie in one row, that region's queen will be in that row. So no other region can use that row: cross out the rest of it. The same works for columns.

3. Cross out squares that every option touches

When a region has only two or three open squares left, check which squares touch all of them. Wherever that region's queen ends up, it will touch those squares, so they can be crossed out.

Worked example: solving a 5×5 Queens puzzle step by step

Follow along with a pencil. Queens shown in orange are the ones we have just placed. Rows are numbered 1 to 5 from the top, and columns 1 to 5 from the left.

  1. Here is the puzzle: five rows, five columns and five colour regions. Notice the tiny region in the top-left corner: it is just one square.

  2. A one-square region. The corner region has only one square, so its queen goes there. Cross out row 1, column 1 and the square that touches it diagonally.

  3. A squeezed region. The top-right region has lost its row 1 squares. Its last two squares (outlined) are both in row 2, so its queen is in row 2. Cross out the other open square in row 2.

  4. Squares every option touches. Whichever of those two squares gets the queen, it will touch row 3, columns 4 and 5. Cross them out.

  5. Another squeeze. The right-hand region now only has squares in column 5, so column 5's queen is in that region. That rules out row 2, column 5, and the top-right region's queen must go in row 2, column 4. Cross out around it.

  6. Squeeze again. The bottom region's open squares are now all in row 5, so row 5 belongs to it. That rules out row 5, column 5, so the right-hand region's queen goes in row 4, column 5. Cross out the rest of row 4.

  7. Finish. The big middle region has just one open square left: row 3, column 2. Placing that queen rules out row 5, column 2, so the last queen goes in row 5, column 3. Every row, column and region now has one queen, and none of them touch.

Common Queens mistakes

  • Forgetting the diagonals. Queens may not touch at the corners either. After placing a queen, cross out all eight squares around it.
  • Not marking crosses. Keeping track in your head works for a few moves, then falls apart. Small crosses make the next step jump out.
  • Only thinking about rows. Columns and regions give just as many clues. Check all three after every queen.
  • Guessing. If you're stuck, look for a region with only two or three open squares and ask which squares they all touch.

Queens questions and answers

Do I need to know chess?

No. The queens are just markers. The only rules are one per row, column and region, and no touching.

Is Queens the same as Star Battle?

They are very close relatives. Star Battle uses stars instead of queens, and harder versions ask for two or more stars in every row, column and region. Queens is the one-per-region version.

What size should a beginner start with?

A 5×5 or 6×6 grid is perfect for learning. Move up to 8×8 and beyond once crossing out feels automatic.

Does every Queens puzzle have only one solution?

A well-made puzzle does. The puzzles in this guide were checked by computer to confirm there is exactly one solution.
