How to Solve Slitherlink Puzzles: Rules and Tips for Beginners

Updated 1 November 2026

Slitherlink is a logic puzzle played on a grid of dots. Your goal is to draw one closed loop along the grid lines, so that each number shows how many of its square's four sides the loop uses. It is also known as Loop the Loop. You never need to guess: every line can be worked out from the numbers.

In short. Cross out every side of a 0, then look for 3s next to a 0 or in a corner. After that, follow the loop: every dot has two lines or none, so a line that reaches a dot with only one way on must take it.

What is Slitherlink?

Slitherlink comes from the Japanese puzzle publisher Nikoli. The board is a square grid of dots, and some of the squares between the dots hold a number from 0 to 3. You draw short lines between neighbouring dots until they join up into a single loop, like a fence around a field.

It helps to mark the sides you know are not on the loop with a small cross. Crosses are as useful as lines: they tell you where the loop can't go.

Slitherlink rules

  1. Draw lines between neighbouring dots, across or down, to make one closed loop.
  2. The loop never crosses itself and never branches. So every dot has either two lines or none.
  3. A number tells how many of its square's four sides the loop uses.
  4. A square with no number can use any number of its sides.

Three Slitherlink solving techniques

1. Start with the 0s

A 0 means none of its four sides is on the loop, so cross them all out. A 3 next to a 0 then has only three sides left, and it needs all of them.

2. Look for a 3 in a corner

The dot in a corner of the board has only two sides. The loop either uses both of them or neither. A 3 in that corner can leave out only one of its sides, so it can't leave out both: the loop uses both corner sides.

3. Follow the loop: two lines or none

Every dot has two lines or none. When a line reaches a dot and all but one of the other ways are crossed out, the loop must go that way. And when a dot already has two lines, cross out its other sides.

Worked example: solving a 4×4 Slitherlink step by step

Follow along with a pencil. Rows are numbered 1 to 4 from the top, and columns 1 to 4 from the left.

  1. Here is the puzzle: two 0s, two 3s and a 1.

  2. The 0s. Cross out all four sides of both 0s.

  3. A 3 next to a 0. The 3 in row 2 sits on top of a 0, so its bottom side is crossed out. It uses its other three sides.

  4. A 3 in a corner. The 3 in the bottom-left corner uses both of its corner sides.

  5. Follow the loop. The two lines coming down from the 3 in row 2 reach the top corners of the 0 below. Going down or along the 0 is crossed out, so the left line turns left and the right line turns right.

  6. Join on the left. The left edge in row 3 starts at a dot whose other two sides belong to the first 0, so it's a dead end: cross it out. The loop at the corner 3 then turns along its top, and the line from step 5 comes down to meet it. The 3 now has its three sides.

  7. Finish. The top row has no way in, so all of it is crossed out. The bottom line runs on under the 1, which then has its one side, and up the right edge to meet the line from step 5. The loop is closed.

Common Slitherlink mistakes

  • Making two loops. The answer is one single loop. If two pieces close up separately, something went wrong.
  • Letting a line stop. A line that reaches a dot must carry on. Only a dot with no lines at all is off the loop.
  • Not crossing out. Crosses are what make the next move visible. Cross out every side you know is not on the loop.
  • Forgetting the empty squares. A square with no number has no limit, but its sides still follow the dot rule.
  • Guessing. A well-made puzzle always has a next step. Look again at the numbers next to your newest lines.

Slitherlink questions and answers

Is Slitherlink the same as Loop the Loop?

Yes. Loop the Loop, Fences and Loopy are other names for the same puzzle, and the rules are the same.

Do I need to be good at maths?

No. The numbers only go up to 3, and you only count the sides of one square at a time.

What size should a beginner start with?

A 5×5 grid is a good start. Move to larger grids when the corner and 0 patterns feel automatic.

Does every Slitherlink puzzle have only one solution?

A well-made puzzle does. The puzzles in this guide were checked by computer to confirm there is exactly one solution.
